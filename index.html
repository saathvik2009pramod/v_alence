Published field evidence / Safelite Glass

When changing the pay rule
changed the work.

Safelite Glass moved installers from hourly wages to a piece-rate system with a guaranteed minimum. Edward Lazear studied the transition using company personnel and output records.

Productivity rose. The analysis attributed the improvement to both changed effort and changes in the workforce attracted and retained by the pay system.

The lesson for incentive design is to examine behaviour alongside payout cost. A rule can change how people work and who chooses to participate. Valence’s model explores one narrow mechanism: discounting to bring a deal forward.

This is a historical research case. The study evaluates Safelite’s pay change and does not test Valence.

Read Lazear, Performance Pay and Productivity

American Economic Review, 90(5), 1346–1361, 2000.

Assumptions & interpretation

Inspect the mechanism.

Two choices, two quarters

Wait: the deal closes next quarter at list price with the selected probability. Bring forward: it closes this quarter with probability 20% + 2.5 percentage points per 1% discount, capped at 95%. If it fails to close early, it can still close next quarter at list price with the same baseline probability. This response to discount is an illustrative assumption.

What enters pay and company contribution?

Sales commission is 3% of revenue. Current bookings are fixed; next-quarter other bookings are assumed zero. Rep pay includes commission and the incremental current-quarter bonus. Company contribution deducts fixed deal costs, commission, and incremental bonus. Both choices are evaluated across the two quarters without time discounting. Expected changes are bring-forward minus wait; a negative company value means a loss under these assumptions.

Three payout structures

Threshold: the target bonus pays at 100% attainment. Accelerator: bonus increases linearly from zero at 90% attainment to the target at 110%, continuing above it. Smooth: bonus rises from zero at 90% to the target at 110% along a cubic Bézier with normalized control points (0,0), (⅓,0), (⅔,1), (1,1), then stays capped. The threshold retains its genuine discontinuity.

What the search establishes

The search compares discounts from 0% to 30% in 0.5 percentage-point steps against waiting. It reports the highest modeled expected rep pay, plus the best modeled company choice. This bounded search demonstrates incentives within the stated model. It does not predict a particular employee’s actions or establish a globally optimal compensation plan.
