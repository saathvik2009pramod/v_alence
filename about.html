About Valence / Saathvik Pramod

A question
worth building for.

I’m a high-school student interested in economics, programming, and the decisions people make inside systems.

Where the question started

What do the rules
actually encourage?

Valence grew out of my interest in how people respond to the systems built around them. Studying economics has given me a way to think about incentives. Programming has given me a way to make those ideas tangible and test the assumptions behind them.

My projects have become a way to investigate questions I encounter in economics. I built Ceteris to help students construct and explore economic diagrams, and Causal Playground to examine how different statistical methods can produce different answers from the same underlying model.

From theory to commercial decisions

The details
change the outcome.

Alongside those projects, I’ve been researching consumer algorithms and contract complexity. That work explores how better computational tools might change the incentives of businesses whose offers they evaluate.

During my logistics internship with Kochi Holdings, I worked on market-entry comparisons involving customs arrangements, duty deferral, and working capital. It gave me experience examining how commercial decisions depend on the details of their terms.

A company sets a target.
A salesperson responds to the reward.
Where do their interests diverge?

Why I built Valence

Make a trade-off
visible.

Valence brings these interests together around compensation design. I wanted to explore the conditions under which a sensible decision for a salesperson becomes an expensive one for the company.

The current prototype investigates that question through models of commissions, discounts, and deal timing. Building it as a high-school student has also meant making the limits explicit. The calculations can demonstrate an incentive conflict under defined assumptions; understanding a real organization takes further evidence.

My aim is to make those trade-offs visible, with calculations people can inspect and assumptions they can challenge.
